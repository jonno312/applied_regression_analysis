103.1 Problem 1

Suppose I recruit a sample of \(n\) teachers to study the effect of hydration and sleep on voice quality. Define the following variables:

  • \(H_i\) is the harmonics-to-noise ratio (HNR) of the \(i\)th participant’s voice. This is a measure of voice quality and is measured in decibels.
  • \(S_i = 1\) if participant \(i\) slept well the night before they were assessed and \(S_i = 0\) if they slept poorly.
  • \(W_i\) is the number of liters of water the \(i\)th participant drank in the previous 24 hours.

Suppose I fit the following model: \[ H_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 S_i + \beta_2 W_i + \beta_3 S_i W_i + \epsilon_i \text{.} \] Please answer the following questions, assuming all necessary assumptions are met.

103.1.1 Part (a)

(4 points) What does \(\beta_0\) mean in real-world terms?

TipSolution

The expected (or average, or mean, or estimated; 1 point) HNR (1 point) for a teacher who slept poorly the night before (1 point) and drank no water (1 point) in the past 24 hours.

103.1.2 Part (b)

(7 points) Compute \(E(H_i|S_i = 1,W_i = w) - E(H_i|S_i = 0,W_i = w)\) and interpret its meaning in real-world terms.

TipSolution

\[ \begin{align*} &E(H_i|S_i = 1,W_i = w) - E(H_i|S_i = 0,W_i = w) \\ &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 (1) + \beta_2 w + \beta_3 (1) w - \left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 (0) + \beta_2 w + \beta_3 (0) w\right) \\ &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 w + \beta_3 w - \left(\beta_0 + \beta_2 w \right) \\ &= \beta_1 + \beta_3 w \text{ (1 point)} \end{align*} \]

The expected (or average, or mean, or estimated; 1 point) difference (1 point) in HNR (1 point) between those who slept well (1 point) and those who slept poorly (1 point), among people who drank \(w\) liters of water (1 point) over the past 24 hours.

103.1.3 Part (c)

(7 points) Compute \(E(H_1 |S_i = 0,W_i = w + 1) - E(H_i|S_i = 0,W_i = w)\) and interpret its meaning in real-world terms.

TipSolution

\[ \begin{align*} &E(H_1 |S_i = 0,W_i = w + 1) - E(H_i|S_i = 0,W_i = w) \\ &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 (0) + \beta_2 (w+1) + \beta_3 (0) (w+1) - \left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 (0) + \beta_2 (w) + \beta_3 (0) w \right) \\ &= \beta_0 + \beta_2 w + \beta_2 - \left(\beta_0 + \beta_2 w \right) \\ &= \beta_2 \text{ (1 point)} \end{align*} \]

The expected (or average, or mean, or estimated; 1 point) change (or increase; 1 point) in HNR (1 point) associated with a one-liter increase (1 point) in water consumption (1 point), among people who slept poorly (1 point).

Alternatively, the expected (or average, or mean, or estimated; 1 point) difference (1 point) in HNR (1 point) between two populations of teachers that differ by one liter (1 point) in their water consumption (1 point) but who all slept poorly (1 point).

103.1.4 Part (d)

Compute \(E(H_1 |S_i = 1,W_i = w + 1) - E(H_i|S_i = 1,W_i = w)\) and interpret its meaning in real-world terms.

TipSolution

\[ \begin{align*} &E(H_1 |S_i = 1,W_i = w + 1) - E(H_i|S_i = 1,W_i = w) \\ &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 (1) + \beta_2 (w+1) + \beta_3 (1) (w+1) - \left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 (1) + \beta_2 (w) + \beta_3 (1) w \right) \\ &= \beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 w + \beta_2 + \beta_3 w + \beta_3 - \left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 + \beta_2 w + \beta_3 w \right) \\ &= \beta_2 + \beta_3 \text{ (1 point)} \end{align*} \]

The expected (or average, or mean, or estimated; 1 point) change (or increase; 1 point) in HNR (1 point) associated with a one-liter increase (1 point) in water consumption (1 point), among people who slept well (1 point).

Alternatively, the expected (or average, or mean, or estimated; 1 point) difference (1 point) in HNR (1 point) between two populations of teachers that differ by one liter (1 point) in their water consumption (1 point) but who all slept well (1 point).

103.2 Problem 2

Please identify each of the following statements as true or false. Each problem is worth one point. You do not need to justify your answers.

103.2.1 Part (a)

We usually consider parameters to be unknown.

TipSolution

True.

103.2.2 Part (b)

A statistic is a function of the population.

TipSolution

False.

103.2.3 Part (c)

We use the sample to draw conclusions about the population.

TipSolution

True.

103.2.4 Part (d)

If you know the expected value of \(X\), you should be able to compute the probability that \(X\) is in any given range.

TipSolution

False.

103.2.5 Part (e)

A statistic can have a distribution.

TipSolution

True.

103.2.6 Part (f)

“Statistic” is another word for “parameter.”

TipSolution

False.
