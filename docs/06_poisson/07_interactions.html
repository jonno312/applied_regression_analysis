82  Interactions

82.1 Learning Objective

Interpret the coefficients of a Poisson regression model with an interaction term.

82.2 Lesson

Let’s return to the data set examining text messages sent by high school seniors in Pennsylvania in 2023 and 2024. These are the variables:

  • TextsSent: Number of text messages sent on the previous day.
  • Gender: Male or female.
  • HangHours: Hours spent hanging out with friends in the previous week.

The results below are for a quasi-Poisson regression with an interaction between gender and hours spent hanging out.


Call:
glm(formula = TextsSent ~ Gender * HangHours, family = quasipoisson, 
    data = d)

How do we interpret the coefficients? In service of answering that question, let’s look at the model: \[ \begin{align*} &\log \hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale},\texttt{HangHours}\right) \\ &= 3.966289 -0.225848\ \texttt{GenderMale} + 0.012592\ \texttt{HangHours} - 0.009417\ \texttt{GenderMale} \times \texttt{HangHours} \text{.} \end{align*} \] This implies that \[ \begin{align*} &\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale},\texttt{HangHours}\right) \\ &= \exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848\ \texttt{GenderMale} + 0.012592\ \texttt{HangHours} - 0.009417\ \texttt{GenderMale} \times \texttt{HangHours}\right) \text{.} \end{align*} \] So, we actually need to exponentiate the coefficients before interpreting them.

exp(mod_01$coefficients)
         (Intercept)           GenderMale            HangHours 
          52.7882917            0.7978395            1.0126721 
GenderMale:HangHours 
           0.9906268

Now we can interpret the coefficients.

  • 52.7882917 is the expected number of text messages sent in one day by a female senior who spent zero hours hanging out with friends the previous week. \[ \begin{align*} &\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 0,\texttt{HangHours} = 0\right) \\ &= \exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (0) + 0.012592 (0) - 0.009417 (0) \times (0)\right) \\ &= \exp\left(3.966289 \right) \\ &= 52.7882917 \text{.} \end{align*} \] In case you were wondering, zero is a reasonable number of hours to have spent hanging out with friends. Seven of the girls in the data set said they spent zero hours hanging out with friends in the previous week.
[1] 7
  • Among seniors who spent zero hours hanging out with friends in the previous week, boys sent about 20% fewer text messages per day than girls. \[ \begin{align*} \frac{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 1,\texttt{HangHours} = 0\right)}{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 0,\texttt{HangHours} = 0\right)} &= \frac{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (1) + 0.012592 (0) - 0.009417 (1) \times (0)\right)}{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (0) + 0.012592 (0) - 0.009417 (0) \times (0) \right)} \\ &= \exp\left(-0.225848\right) \\ &= 0.7978395 \text{.} \end{align*} \]
  • Among girls, each additional hour spent hanging out with friends is associated with a 1.26721% increase in the number of text messages sent per day. \[ \begin{align*} \frac{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 0,\texttt{HangHours} = x+1\right)}{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 0,\texttt{HangHours} = x\right)} &= \frac{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (0) + 0.012592 (x+1) - 0.009417 (0) \times (x+1)\right)}{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (0) + 0.012592 x - 0.009417 (0) \times x \right)} \\ &= \exp(0.012592) \\ &= 1.0126721 \text{.} \end{align*} \]
  • Among seniors who spent \(x\) hours hanging out with friends in the previous week, boys sent \(\exp(-0.225848-0.009417x)\) as many text messages per day as girls. \[ \begin{align*} \frac{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 1,\texttt{HangHours} = x\right)}{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 0,\texttt{HangHours} = x\right)} &= \frac{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (1) + 0.012592 x - 0.009417 (1) \times x\right)}{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (0) + 0.012592 x - 0.009417 (0) \times x \right)} \\ &= \exp(-0.225848-0.009417x) \\ \end{align*} \]
  • Among boys, each additional hour spent hanging out with friends is associated with a 0.318% increase in the number of text messages sent per day. \[ \begin{align*} \frac{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 1,\texttt{HangHours} = x+1\right)}{\hat{E}\left(\texttt{TextsSent}\middle|\texttt{GenderMale} = 1,\texttt{HangHours} = x\right)} &= \frac{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (1) + 0.012592 (x+1) - 0.009417 (1) \times (x+1)\right)}{\exp\left(3.966289 -0.225848 (1) + 0.012592 x - 0.009417 (1) \times x \right)} \\ &= \exp\left(0.012592-0.009417\right) \\ &= 1.00318 \text{.} \end{align*} \]

82.3 Problems

82.3.1 Problem 1

This data set comes from an experiment in which a student counted the number of ants on bits of different sandwiches. These are the variables:

  • Ants: Number of ants on the sandwich.
  • Butter: yes if there was butter on the sandwich, no if not.
  • Bread: Type of bread, with the following levels:
    • Multigrain
    • Rye
    • White
    • Wholemeal

These are the results from a quasi-Poisson regression of the number of ants on Butter and Bread, with an interaction between the two

mod_02 <- glm(formula = Ants ~ Butter * Bread,
              data = d2,
              family = quasipoisson)
summary(mod_02)
